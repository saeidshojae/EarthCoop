# Persian SEO Phase 4 — External Source Map

Date: 2026-10-06

Status: research/content map only. No public article copy is changed by this document.

## Purpose

This map separates three evidence classes before any citation pass is applied to the eight Wave-1 Persian SEO articles:

1. **External/generic claims** that can benefit from independent authoritative evidence.
2. **EarthCoop-specific definitions, rules and design claims** that must continue to point to official EarthCoop Pillars/Docs rather than being presented as externally established doctrine.
3. **Explanatory or normative EarthCoop framing** where an external citation is optional or unnecessary.

External sources below are recommendations for later editorial review. Listing a source does **not** imply that the source endorses EarthCoop, validates the full EarthCoop model, or establishes an equivalence between EarthCoop and the external concept.

## Source selection rules

- Prefer primary institutions, standards bodies, intergovernmental organisations and strong academic literature.
- Use external evidence only for claims the source actually supports.
- Keep official EarthCoop rules, terminology and configurable mechanics sourced to EarthCoop Docs.
- Do not use an external source to imply that an EarthCoop-specific term is an established academic/legal category.
- Do not add citation clutter where the article is plainly describing EarthCoop's own model.
- Comparison concepts remain comparisons unless the approved entity map says otherwise.

## 1. `people-economy-explained`

**Article:** «اقتصاد مردمی چیست؟ از مشارکت اقتصادی تا اقتصاد آزاد مردمی»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Broad claims that weak competition, concentration or market power can reduce consumer choice, innovation and market dynamism.
- The distinction between broad participation in economic activity and a fully state-run economy is mainly explanatory framing; it does not need a citation unless a specific external model is named.
- References to co-operatives or worker/member ownership may benefit from an institutional source if the public copy is later expanded.

### Recommended external sources

1. **OECD — Competition and market dynamism**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/competition-and-market-dynamism.html  
   Useful for the generic claim that healthy competition supports consumer welfare, innovation and growth, while weaker competition can mean higher prices and less choice.

2. **ILO — Worker cooperatives**  
   https://www.ilo.org/worker-cooperatives  
   Useful only if the article expands its generic discussion of member-owned/member-controlled enterprise forms.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- «اقتصاد آزاد مردمی» as the name of EarthCoop's proposed economic model.
- EarthCoop's combination of legitimate private ownership, common rights in Earth/resources, transparency, anti-monopoly safeguards and public participation.
- EarthCoop project, fund, share/value or investment mechanics.

### Citation decision

**Light external citation pass only.** One OECD citation near the discussion of monopoly/concentration is sufficient. Do not externalise the definition of «اقتصاد آزاد مردمی».

## 2. `free-market-without-monopoly`

**Article:** «بازار آزاد بدون انحصار چگونه ممکن است؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Competition can support lower prices, consumer choice, innovation and market dynamism.
- Concentration and market power are not identical to firm size; competition analysis requires more than a single concentration measure.
- Competition policy can address cartels, abuse of dominance and anti-competitive mergers.

### Recommended external sources

1. **OECD — Competition and market dynamism**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/competition-and-market-dynamism.html  
   Strong primary policy source for the relationship between competition, prices, choice, innovation and market power.

2. **OECD — Competitive and fair markets**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/competitive-and-fair-markets.html  
   Useful for the narrower claim that competitive markets can improve quality, prices and innovation while policy should maintain a level playing field.

3. **OECD Competition Trends 2025**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-competition-trends-2025_8c4bd00b-en.html  
   Optional background if the final copy makes empirical claims about enforcement, dominance or merger intervention.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- EarthCoop's normative boundary between legitimate private property and monopoly/common-right violations.
- The definition of «اقتصاد آزاد مردمی».
- Any EarthCoop implementation mechanism for transparency, market access, projects or collective investment.

### Citation decision

**External citations recommended.** This article makes the strongest generic competition-policy claims in Wave 1 and can benefit from 1–2 OECD citations.

## 3. `financial-transparency-and-privacy`

**Article:** «شفافیت مالی و حریم خصوصی چگونه همزمان ممکن‌اند؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Transparency/accountability does not require indiscriminate publication of personal data.
- Data minimisation, purpose limitation, integrity/confidentiality and accountable processing are recognised privacy principles.
- Audit/logging and access controls can support accountability without making all underlying personal data public.

### Recommended external sources

1. **European Data Protection Board — Basic principles**  
   https://www.edpb.europa.eu/topics/key-gdpr-concepts/basic-principles_en  
   Strong source for lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, integrity/confidentiality and accountability.

2. **NIST Privacy Framework**  
   https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework  
   Useful for privacy-risk management and the principle that data processing should be managed deliberately rather than by indiscriminate disclosure.

3. **NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 — Data Processing Management**  
   https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2020/01/16/NIST%20Privacy%20Framework_V1.0.pdf  
   Especially relevant if the public copy later makes the technical claim that audit/log records should be designed alongside data-minimisation controls.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- «اقتصاد شیشه‌ای» as EarthCoop terminology.
- The «بانک شفاف / شیشه مقاوم» metaphor.
- Which EarthCoop financial fields are public, member-visible, auditor-visible or private.
- Actual access-control, retention or audit rules implemented by EarthCoop.

### Citation decision

**External citations recommended.** EDPB plus one NIST source is enough; do not imply that GDPR/NIST defines «اقتصاد شیشه‌ای».

## 4. `participatory-governance-beyond-voting`

**Article:** «حکمرانی مشارکتی چیست و چرا فقط رأی دادن کافی نیست؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Citizen participation can include more than elections: consultation, monitoring, deliberation, participatory budgeting and other forms of input.
- Meaningful participation requires room for real influence, feedback, transparency and accountability.
- Institutionalised participation can extend beyond one-off voting events.

### Recommended external sources

1. **OECD Guidelines for Citizen Participation Processes**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-citizen-participation-processes_f765caf6-en.html  
   Primary source for multiple participation methods and quality principles such as transparency, accountability, inclusion, privacy and evaluation.

2. **OECD — Eight ways to institutionalise deliberative democracy**  
   https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/eight-ways-to-institutionalise-deliberative-democracy_4fcf1da5-en.html  
   Useful for the generic claim that democratic participation can be institutionally embedded rather than limited to isolated events.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- EarthCoop's group/assembly hierarchy from local levels upward.
- The distinction between active participation at the base level and observation/representation at higher levels.
- Manager/inspector roles, voting rights, delegation mechanics and responsibility rules.
- The statement that AI/algorithms assist but do not become the human rights-holder or sovereign decision-maker within EarthCoop.

### Citation decision

**External citations recommended.** OECD provides a strong independent foundation for the generic participation discussion; EarthCoop mechanics remain separately sourced.

## 5. `continuous-elections-explained`

**Article:** «انتخابات مستمر چیست و چه مسئله‌ای را حل می‌کند؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- The definition of **liquid democracy** as combining direct participation with delegable/reversible representation.
- The fact that liquid democracy is a distinct external concept and should not be used as a synonym for every continuous or dynamic voting mechanism.

### Recommended external sources

1. **Springer — E-Democracy: Conceptual Foundations and Recent Trends**  
   https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-27184-8_2  
   Academic background describing liquid democracy as bridging direct and representative democracy through conditional/reversible delegation.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- «انتخابات دائمی بدون نامزد» as EarthCoop's own model.
- Research periods, stop points, acceptance/replacement rules, manager/inspector roles and configurable thresholds.
- Topic delegation rules and non-redelegation mechanics.
- The balance EarthCoop chooses between continuity, accountability and operational stability.

### Citation decision

**One external comparison citation is enough.** Do not seek an external source to "prove" EarthCoop's continuous-election model; the authoritative evidence for that model is EarthCoop's own election law and implementation.

## 6. `platform-cooperative-and-earthcoop`

**Article:** «تعاونی پلتفرمی چیست و چه نسبتی با EarthCoop دارد؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Platform cooperatives are member-owned/cooperatively controlled businesses using digital platforms, applications or protocols.
- Worker/member democratic ownership and control distinguish cooperative models from conventional investor-controlled platforms.
- Digital labour platforms create both opportunities and governance/work-condition challenges.

### Recommended external sources

1. **ILO COOP/SSE 100 Symposium — Platform Cooperatives**  
   https://www.ilo.org/ilo-coopsse-100-symposium-session-5-platform-cooperatives  
   Direct institutional definition of platform cooperatives as member-owned businesses using websites/apps/protocols with democratic ownership and control.

2. **ILO — Worker cooperatives**  
   https://www.ilo.org/worker-cooperatives  
   Useful for the broader member-worker-owner democratic governance context.

3. **ILO — Digital labour platforms**  
   https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/digital-labour-platforms  
   Optional background for the wider platform economy and its opportunities/challenges.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- EarthCoop is **not** asserted to be identical to a conventional platform cooperative.
- EarthCoop's local-to-global assemblies, general/professional/special groups, economic system, common-right theory and governance architecture.
- Any legal classification of EarthCoop in a particular jurisdiction.

### Citation decision

**High-priority external citation pass.** This article explicitly defines and compares an external established concept, so the ILO definition should be cited. The comparison boundary must remain explicit.

## 7. `earth-in-earthcoop-justice`

**Article:** «چرا زمین در نظریه عدالت EarthCoop جایگاه بنیادی دارد؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

Most of this article is intentionally EarthCoop's own philosophical/legal framing. External sources should not be used to make the EarthCoop definition of justice look universally accepted.

Optional external support is appropriate only for adjacent background claims:

- shared natural resources can be governed through institutions other than pure privatisation or central command;
- environmental conditions and human rights are interrelated.

### Recommended external sources

1. **Nobel Prize / Royal Swedish Academy — Elinor Ostrom, Economic Governance and the Commons**  
   https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/press-release/  
   Useful only for the adjacent proposition that common resources can be successfully governed by user associations under suitable institutions.

2. **UNEP — What are environmental rights?**  
   https://www.unep.org/explore-topics/environmental-rights-and-governance/what-we-do/advancing-environmental-rights/what  
   Optional background for the relationship between human rights, environmental conditions, participation and access to justice.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- EarthCoop's definition of justice as placing each thing/right in its proper place and delivering each right to its rightful holder.
- The foundational common-right claim concerning Earth and resources.
- The relationship EarthCoop establishes among common rights, private property, responsible freedom and foundational equality.
- «حق مالکانه همگانی» and all operational/economic consequences.

### Citation decision

**External citation optional and restrained.** The article is principally about EarthCoop's own theory; one adjacent Ostrom/UNEP citation may add context but should not be presented as validation of the EarthCoop doctrine.

## 8. `private-property-and-common-resources`

**Article:** «مالکیت خصوصی و منابع مشترک چگونه با هم جمع می‌شوند؟»

### External/generic claims worth supporting

- Commons/common-pool resources can be governed through diverse institutional arrangements and are not reducible to a simple private-versus-state binary.
- Clear rules, monitoring and conflict-resolution mechanisms are recurrent features in successful commons governance research.
- Environmental-resource governance can involve rights, duties, participation and equitable benefit considerations.

### Recommended external sources

1. **Nobel Prize / Royal Swedish Academy — Elinor Ostrom press release**  
   https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/press-release/  
   Strong concise source for successful user governance of common property and rule-based management of shared resources.

2. **Nobel Prize — Elinor Ostrom facts**  
   https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/facts/  
   Useful background on empirical studies of shared natural-resource governance.

3. **UNEP — Framework Principles on Human Rights and the Environment**  
   https://www.unep.org/resources/policy-and-strategy/framework-principles-human-rights-and-environment  
   Optional source when discussing participation, protection of environmental rights and fair/equitable benefit issues in natural-resource contexts.

### EarthCoop-specific claims that must stay with EarthCoop Docs

- Legitimate private ownership of the fruits of labour as defined by EarthCoop.
- EarthCoop's exact distinction between private property and common rights in Earth/resources.
- «حق مالکانه همگانی», its purpose, rates, formulas, funds and distribution mechanisms.
- The statement that EarthCoop's solution is neither blanket confiscation nor unrestricted private appropriation.

### Citation decision

**Selective external citation recommended.** Ostrom is useful for the generic commons-governance background; the normative property-right synthesis remains EarthCoop's own doctrine.

## Public-copy gate

Before changing any seeded/public article:

1. review the exact sentence the external source would support;
2. ensure the citation does not imply endorsement of EarthCoop;
3. keep EarthCoop-specific definitions/rules linked to official EarthCoop Docs;
4. preserve the approved comparison boundaries in `persian-topic-entity-map.md`;
5. avoid adding unstable numbers or configurable system parameters;
6. make citation changes in a separate reviewed content pass.
