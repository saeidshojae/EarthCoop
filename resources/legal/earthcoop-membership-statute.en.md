# EarthCoop International Membership Statute
**Document ID:** EC-MEM | **Publication edition:** 1.0 | **Status:** Initial publication text for member review | **Source language:** Persian

## Preamble
In the name of the God of life and wisdom.

EarthCoop is a voluntary, multi-level partnership platform established to advance justice, organize participation and public oversight, and create conditions for the fair enjoyment of land and shared resources. In EarthCoop’s foundational understanding, justice means “putting everything in its proper place and giving every right to its rightful holder.” The Earth and the resources essential to life are matters of a common right; at the same time, legitimate private ownership of each person’s labor and creativity is respected. Respect for the rights of living beings, ecosystems, and future generations is part of this shared responsibility.

Under EarthCoop’s principles, all human beings share, before any registration, an inherent common right concerning the Earth and its fundamental resources. The platform recognizes this inherent and potential partnership and provides a voluntary membership path through which organized participation may become active.

This Statute is intended to ensure that, before joining, each person understands EarthCoop’s purpose, the rights membership provides, and the obligations it entails. Accepting it means agreeing to the platform’s membership rules; it does not require acceptance of any personal or religious belief.

## Article 1 — Nature and legal limits
1. EarthCoop is a platform for cooperation, organizing members, and the gradual formation of partnership institutions from local to global levels.
2. Expressions such as “partnership republic,” “partnership citizenship,” or “popular sovereignty” in EarthCoop documents describe an organizational model and an aspiration internal to the platform. By themselves, they do not confer state citizenship, public sovereign authority recognized under international law, registered ownership of land, or registered legal-person status.
3. Contractual rights and obligations arise in relation to the legal person operating the platform, whose valid identity and contact details must be stated on the documents page before public publication. Each affiliated body is responsible only within its announced authority and commitments.
4. EarthCoop’s development and activities shall be peaceful, voluntary, and conducted within the framework of applicable laws.

## Article 2 — Fundamental principles
1. All people have equal dignity and equal opportunity to participate in the fundamental right to fair enjoyment of the Earth and shared resources.
2. Legitimate private ownership of the products of labor, creativity, production, services, capital, and intellectual works is respected. Exclusive use of shared resources must be compatible with the rights of others and the protection of nature.
3. Executive authority is entrusted by the members; no officeholder, investor, or technology is beyond oversight and accountability.
4. Diversity of beliefs, languages, cultures, and peaceful viewpoints is respected.

## Article 2 bis — Potential global partnership and active membership
1. Under EarthCoop’s justice-based principles, all human beings, whether registered or not, have a share and an entitlement in the common right concerning the Earth and the fundamental resources of life by virtue of being human. EarthCoop does not grant this right; it recognizes it.
2. The terms “partner” or “potential shareholder in the global partnership” express fundamental equality in the common right and the possibility of participation. By themselves, these terms do not mean registered shares in a company, registered ownership of parcels of land, transferable assets, an enforceable claim to profits, or a contractual relationship with people who have not registered.
3. Voluntary registration and acceptance of the documents recognize potential partnership within EarthCoop’s membership structure and bring it into an active stage. Full participation and voting rights remain subject to completion of membership steps and transparent, valid conditions.
4. A person’s decision not to register does not deprive them of the fundamental right recognized in EarthCoop’s principles and creates no contractual, financial, or political obligation for them. Active membership must not negate the fundamental rights of people outside the platform.
5. An equal share in the fundamental common right is distinct from value shares, investment, participation points, Bahar, or any specific contractual asset. None of these instruments is the source of that fundamental right or a measure of human superiority.

## Article 3 — Voluntary membership
1. Membership is based on a freely made request, informed acceptance of the required documents, and completion of the registration steps.
2. Initial registration takes place by accepting this Statute and the Terms of Use, creating an account, and verifying an email address. This stage is called “Basic User” and does not confer full participation rights.
3. Conversion to “Full Member” requires completion of the prescribed identity, occupational/professional, and location data, the required verifications, and relevant legal requirements.
4. As a general rule, each person has one valid identity account. Identity-determination errors must be open to challenge and correction.

## Article 4 — Information and membership groups
1. Based on member data, the platform assigns membership across three group families: public geographic assemblies; occupational and professional assemblies; and permitted specialized groups.
2. The geographic structure follows existing administrative divisions, from the basic community level—including a neighborhood, district, a city without lower-level divisions, or a village without neighborhoods—up to global levels.
3. A member participates actively according to their assigned role in their valid basic community and ordinarily has observer status at higher levels, unless selected, represented, or granted a specific valid authorization.
4. A proposed location’s registration and its placement under review do not, by themselves, mean that the location has been finally approved. The status of related groups must be displayed clearly.
5. Voluntary membership in optional micro-location groups is subject to those groups’ settings and conditions.

## Article 5 — Members’ rights
1. To know the current rules, membership role and status, reasons for important decisions, and material changes.
2. To participate, vote, make proposals, and stand for or elect candidates within the authority of the active role and the valid rules of the relevant group.
3. To inspect and oversee public matters, budgets, and the performance of managers and inspectors to the extent they may be disclosed, without infringing others’ privacy.
4. To access and correct personal data and request a review, subject to law and audit requirements.
5. To criticize, complain, defend oneself, and request reconsideration of restrictions and important decisions.
6. To leave membership and receive an explanation of the effects on obligations, records, and any potential financial rights.

## Article 6 — Participation and elections
1. Main group elections are conducted periodically or continuously, in accordance with valid procedures and announced settings, based on nominations of trusted people for management and inspection, without requiring a candidate.
2. Quorums, the number of managers and inspectors, terms, voting rules, delegation, and acceptance of responsibilities may change under adopted rules and must be visible to members when they act.
3. Holding the position of manager or inspector does not, by itself, remove an individual’s voting right in a group where they have that right as an active member.
4. Observer members may participate directly in higher-level decisions only within announced permissions. Where the procedures so specify, the “temporary active” role does not necessarily confer voting rights or affect an election quorum.
5. Casting or withdrawing a vote and configuring ballot confidentiality are governed by the current options and procedures. Vote-buying, coercion, forgery, and manipulation are prohibited.

## Article 7 — Member responsibilities
A member undertakes to provide and update information honestly; respect others’ rights, privacy, labor, and legitimate property; refrain from deception, threats, hate speech, fraud, manipulation of votes or participation points, and financial abuse; and comply with applicable laws and the platform’s announced rules when using its services.

## Article 8 — Responsibilities of managers and inspectors
1. A management, inspection, or representative role does not create ownership rights over a group or members’ assets.
2. Accepting any such role requires separate acceptance of the announced duties, powers, conflicts-of-interest rules, and financial terms.
3. Officeholders must be accountable, subject to oversight, and governed by arrangements for removal and replacement.

## Article 8 bis — Cooperation with governments, public institutions, and technology companies
1. To pursue its peaceful objectives, EarthCoop may engage in dialogue, agreements, and lawful, transparent cooperation with governments, municipalities, international organizations, public institutions, universities, cooperatives, and companies. Cooperation with an external body does not imply political dependence or transfer of the members’ partnership governance.
2. Cooperation with governments does not mean claiming to replace a government, exercising public sovereign authority, violating applicable law, or unconditionally accepting every external demand. The legal limits of cooperation and the internal independence of institutions must be clear and reviewable.
3. Cooperation with technology companies, infrastructure providers, artificial-intelligence providers, and data processors is permitted only under clear agreements addressing intellectual property, security, confidentiality, access levels, data use, portability, auditability, service continuity, liability for harm, and termination of cooperation.
4. No government, technology supplier, or commercial partner acquires a special vote, ownership of members’ personal data, or permission to manipulate elections, financial accounts, the participation-point system, or partnership decisions merely by virtue of cooperation. Access to or disclosure of information must be based on a valid legal basis and comply with necessity, proportionality, and individuals’ rights.
5. Agreements with significant public impact must, to the extent permitted by law and while protecting legitimate trade secrets and privacy, be transparent, subject to oversight, and include a process for addressing conflicts of interest.

## Article 9 — Data, transparency, and privacy
1. Identity, location, financial, and participation data may be processed only for specified purposes and on a valid basis. Details shall be provided in a separate Privacy Notice.
2. Transparency in public affairs does not authorize unrestricted disclosure of personal information, confidential ballots, or sensitive documents.
3. Automated decisions affecting a member’s fundamental rights must, to the extent technically and legally possible, be explainable and subject to human review.

## Article 10 — Financial matters and Najm Bahar
1. Recognition of a potential common right and active membership in EarthCoop do not, by themselves, create a financial account, withdrawable credit, registered share of land, company shares, or a guaranteed receivable. Specific financial effects arise only under separate agreements and valid laws.
2. Opening an account and using Najm Bahar require separate acceptance of its financial and monetary agreement.
3. Participating in a project, purchasing a value share, or using a marketplace may require other specific contracts; none automatically creates a special voting right or greater governing authority.

## Article 11 — Exit, suspension, and review
1. A member may request to leave. The legal and financial effects of current agreements, established debts, legally required record retention, and third-party rights are determined under the relevant terms and agreements.
2. Restriction or suspension must have proportionate grounds, notice where feasible, a route to appeal, and fair review. Urgent security measures may be temporary.
3. Disputes shall first be pursued through the announced support and internal review process. This Statute does not remove access to competent legal authorities where the law guarantees that right.

## Article 12 — Higher-level documents and amendments
1. For membership purposes, this Statute is an accessible statement of fundamental rules and shall be interpreted consistently with EarthCoop’s parent document, Charter, Constitution, Executive Statute, and subject-specific laws. Conflicts must be corrected before publication.
2. The version, effective date, text of changes, and approving authority for each amendment must be published. Material amendments affecting member obligations require renewed acceptance where necessary; changes do not automatically or retroactively extinguish accrued rights.
3. Persian is the source text for this proposed edition. Translations must be accurate and accessible, and discrepancies between versions require a clear resolution process, without disregarding mandatory consumer rights.

## Article 13 — Statement of acceptance
“Having read the EarthCoop International Membership Statute, I knowingly request to join this partnership platform and accept the rights, responsibilities, and limitations of membership.”
